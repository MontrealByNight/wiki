# Anarch Territory: Montreal Locations

## St Helens Island
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **La Ronde**: A large amusement park located on Saint Helen's Island, offering a wide range of rides, attractions, and entertainment options. The park is set on the island’s elevated terrain, providing scenic views of Montreal’s skyline and the St. Lawrence River. It features roller coasters, water rides, and a variety of family-friendly attractions. It is a popular destination for both locals and tourists, particularly during the warmer months.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5225, -73.5353`
* **Circuit Gilles-Villeneuve**: A renowned Formula 1 race track located on Saint Helen's Island, known for hosting the Canadian Grand Prix. The track winds through the island’s landscape, with sections that offer challenging turns and high-speed straights. Outside of race events, the circuit is used for recreational activities like cycling and walking, with parts of the track offering panoramic views of the surrounding water.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5039, -73.5268`
* **Stewart Museum**: Located on the western tip of Saint Helen's Island, this museum is housed in a historic military building. It features exhibits related to Montreal's history, the history of the island, and the broader history of New France. The museum’s collection includes artifacts from various periods, with a particular focus on Quebec's colonial past. The surrounding grounds offer a tranquil environment with views of the river and nearby greenery.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5218, -73.5358`
* **Parc Jean-Drapeau**: While technically a larger area encompassing both Saint Helen’s Island and Île Notre-Dame, Parc Jean-Drapeau is a major green space in Montreal. The park offers lush lawns, walking paths, and cultural spaces. It is home to numerous festivals, concerts, and events throughout the year, and the varied landscape includes gardens, sculptures, and sports facilities.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5126, -73.5326`
* **The Asylum**: Welcome to the Asylum, the hidden gem of Saint Helen Island in Montreal, where the night comes alive with a blend of mystery, decadence, and supernatural allure. Nestled within the shadows of the city, this clandestine haven is a playground for creatures of the night, both mortal and immortal alike.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5261, -73.5350`

## Saint Leonard
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Leonardo Da Vinci Centre**: A low-profile cultural complex with clean architecture and wide glass entrances. The halls are lined with local art, and its rooms host anything from language classes to community gatherings. At night, the parking lot is calm, the building’s interior lit dimly through thick windows.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5905, -73.5930`
* **Wilfrid-Bastien Park**: A stretch of green surrounded by quiet residential streets. Its large trees provide a dense canopy, and there’s a small pond in the middle. After dusk, the walking paths are empty, and the playground sits abandoned under pale streetlamps.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5910, -73.5960`
* **Disused Tunnel Lacordaire**: Long sealed off from the public, this old pedestrian tunnel runs beneath the busy boulevard. Cracked tiles and faint traces of graffiti remain, and the walls echo with even the slightest noise. It’s easy to miss, but harder to forget.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5880, -73.5850`
* **Saint-Léonard Cavern Entrance**: Tucked near a modest park, this natural cave system once served as a curiosity and geological site. The surface area is fenced off now, but stories persist of deeper passages that were never fully explored—out of sight and largely ignored by the city above.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5828, -73.6122`

## Montreal East
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Hôpital Vétérinaire**: A squat, utilitarian brick building tucked between industrial lots. Fluorescent lights hum softly inside, casting a sterile glow over clean floors and worn counters. At night, the parking lot sits mostly empty, save for a lone streetlamp flickering overhead.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6358, -73.4985`
    * **Bagger DC**: 2
* **Refinery Overlook**: Set just above the industrial complexes, this small hill offers a wide view of chimneys, pipes, and storage tanks sprawling across the horizon. During the day, it smells of oil and smoke; at night, the whole landscape glows orange with safety lights and scattered flares.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6320, -73.5150`
* **Rue Notre-Dame Industrial Strip**: Lined with aging warehouses and fenced-off service yards, this stretch of road is mostly deserted after sunset. Faint music or machinery can sometimes be heard through thin metal walls. Shadows move strangely between trucks and loading docks.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6200, -73.5050`
* **Waterfront Rail Yard**: Parallel to the St. Lawrence River, the rail yard sees minimal traffic but remains in active use. Rows of freight cars sit still beneath skeletal lights. The air smells faintly of rust and diesel, and graffiti-covered concrete adds an eerie character to the place after dark.

## Mercier Hochelaga Maisonneuve
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Montréal Biodome**: Housed in a distinctive domed structure, this facility contains several controlled ecosystems under one roof—lush tropical forests, coastal cliffs, and polar environments. The air changes with every section, and the winding walkways and overhead platforms give the space a surreal, almost theatrical quality.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5592, -73.5498`
* **Le Repos Saint-François**: A vast cemetery with winding gravel paths, towering trees, and rows of aging mausoleums. Lantern-like lights line the walkways, offering a dim glow in the evening. The quiet is complete, deep, settled, and patient.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5781, -73.5658`
    * **Graverobber DC**: 2
* **Charron Island**: Accessible by bridge but surrounded by the dark waters of the river, this forested island feels like a place out of time. Industrial remnants linger on the fringes, half-consumed by nature. The deeper woods are dense and tangled, rarely disturbed.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5815, -73.4912`
    * **Roadside Killer DC**: 3
* **Marché Maisonneuve**: A large public market housed in a beaux-arts building with brick columns and arched windows. Inside, the tiled floors echo with footsteps and low conversation. By day it’s full of life and at night the closed stalls and empty spaces take on a much different mood.

## Riviere des Prairies
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Bois-de-l'Anjou Nature Park**: A dense stretch of preserved woodland nestled behind suburban streets. Narrow trails wind through groves of pine and birch, and the quiet is heavy under the canopy, broken only by the rustle of wind or the occasional bird call.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6190, -73.5460`
    * **Farmer DC**: 3
* **CLSC Rivière-des-Prairies**: A modest public health facility with flat brick walls and long corridors. The design is utilitarian, a bit dated, with a subdued presence that fits its purpose.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6530, -73.5650`
* **Rivière-des-Prairies Train Station**: A quiet commuter station with a narrow platform and minimal signage. The atmosphere is subdued, especially at night, with little more than the buzz of distant streetlights and the sound of passing trains.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6580, -73.5500`
* **Église Saint-Joseph-de-Rivière-des-Prairies**: A classic Catholic church with a pale stone façade and a single bell tower. Set back from the road with a simple front lawn, it offers a sense of stillness and tradition.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6660, -73.5020`

## Montreal Nord
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Emmanuel Church**: A modest stone structure tucked between residential streets, its sloped roof and simple steeple give it a grounded, familiar presence. The surrounding yard is quiet, with a small iron fence and sparse trees. Inside, the lighting is soft and indirect, and the air holds the faint scent of old wood and silence.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6012, -73.6358`
    * **Cleaver DC**: 4
    * **Extortionist DC**: 3
    * **Montero DC**: 3
    * **Pursuer DC**: 3
    * **Sandman DC**: 4
* **Boulevard Henri-Bourassa Est**: A wide commercial boulevard lined with strip malls, bus stops, and fast food outlets. The traffic is steady, day and night, and the sidewalks are worn from constant use. Occasional alleyways and service entrances break the rhythm of storefronts, leading into narrower, less-traveled spaces.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6050, -73.6200`
* **Parc Pilon**: A small neighborhood park bordered by mid-rise apartment buildings. It features open green space, a few playground structures, and concrete benches under tired trees. In the off-hours, it’s mostly empty, with the sound of wind moving through the chain-link fences and basketball hoops.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6030, -73.6300`
* **Cégep Marie-Victorin**: Set slightly apart from the residential sprawl, this college campus has modern, angular buildings and large windows that reflect the sky. The surrounding grounds are quiet in the evenings, with open courtyards and long corridors lit by flickering hallway lights. The outer parking lots stretch into darker, more deserted corners.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.6148, -73.6191`

## The Gay Village
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Rue Sainte-Catherine Est**: A pedestrian-friendly street lined with cafés, bars, and boutiques. Bright murals cover building walls, and overhead decorations change with the seasons. At night, the area shifts to a vibrant glow of neon, alive with movement and music, yet never losing its layered, urban grit.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5175, -73.5550`
* **Beaudry Metro Station**: A distinct metro entrance with its colorful columns, this station serves as a gateway to the neighborhood. Below ground, tiled corridors and the distant rumble of trains echo with late-night footfalls. Above, it opens onto a busy corner that always seems to hold someone waiting.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5191, -73.5568`
* **Parc de l’Espoir**: A small, triangular park set slightly apart from the main drag. Benches and a modest memorial give the space a quiet, reflective mood. Though surrounded by the city’s pulse, it often feels removed, like time slows slightly here.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5210, -73.5540`
* **The Loft Building**: An old industrial structure converted into residential lofts and occasional studio spaces. The brick façade is marked by metal fire escapes and large windows. Inside, exposed beams and worn wood floors hint at former lives—sometimes silent, sometimes filled with music, conversation, or something stranger.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5230, -73.5500`
