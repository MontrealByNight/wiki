# Contested Territory: Montreal Locations

## The Underground City
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Complexe Desjardins**: A vast, multi-level indoor plaza with polished floors, glass-paneled walls, and a massive central atrium that fills with soft, filtered light by day and a dim glow by night. Wide corridors connect shops, cafés, and passageways that branch out in every direction. In the quiet hours, footsteps echo endlessly here.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5075, -73.5653`
* **Central Station Tunnels**: Below street level, these tunnels connect the metro to shopping centers and office complexes, lined with dimly lit tiles and long corridors. The muted sounds of the city above are barely perceptible here, adding a sense of stillness as pedestrians move quietly along the maze of passages.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.4998, -73.5665`
* **La Cité Complex**: An extensive network that merges underground shops and residential spaces, with dark, labyrinthine hallways and hidden alcoves where escalators seem to disappear into the shadows. Some pathways feel isolated, almost forgotten, creating secluded corners rarely traversed.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5115, -73.5740`
* **St. James the Apostle Chapel**:Nestled within a secluded section of the Underground City, this small, historic chapel has low vaulted ceilings and stone walls, its entrance modest and unassuming. Inside, dim lights flicker, illuminating worn wooden pews and delicate stained-glass windows that cast faint colors onto the floors. Here, there is an almost tangible sense of silence and solitude, as if untouched by the busy life above.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.4975, -73.5760`
* **Square-Victoria Passages**: A maze of quieter, winding corridors near the business district, these passages open into scattered seating areas and occasionally empty intersections. The atmosphere feels like a blend of modern design and dimly lit spaces, with only the faint hum of air conditioning and distant echoes breaking the silence.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5018, -73.5625`

## The Old Port
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Place Jacques-Cartier**: A lively, historic square stretching down toward the river, lined with cobblestone paths and bordered by stone façades under the glow of old-fashioned streetlights. Shadows pool between the buildings, creating hidden nooks amidst the centuries-old architecture.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5074, -73.5534`
    * **Extortionist DC**: 4
* **Dorchester Square**: Dorchester Square is a historic urban park surrounded by elegant and iconic Montreal architecture. The square is meticulously landscaped, with manicured lawns, tall trees, and wide gravel pathways that crisscross the grounds, inviting visitors to stroll or pause on a shaded bench. Statues of historic figures stand in the park’s corners, and the focal point is a grand monument featuring a bronzed lion at its base, watching over the square with silent majesty.
By day, the square is bustling, with the murmur of people and the distant sound of city traffic; by night, it becomes quieter, the streetlamps casting long shadows over the pathways. The square's central location in the heart of downtown makes it a popular, atmospheric spot amid the city's busy pace.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.4996, -73.5702`
* **Nacarat Bar**: Nestled atop the historic Queen Elizabeth hotel, overlooking the bustling streets of Montreal's old port, lies the Nacarat bar—a hidden gem amid the city's skyline. Accessible only to those with an eye for the extraordinary, this rooftop oasis offers a haven of sophistication and allure.
As twilight descends upon the city, the Nacarat bar comes to life, bathed in the soft glow of ambient lighting that casts an enchanting aura over its surroundings. The air is filled with the soulful melodies of live jazz music, drifting lazily through the evening breeze and beckoning patrons to lose themselves in its rhythm.
Beyond the confines of the bar, a spacious rooftop terrace awaits, offering panoramic views of Montreal's skyline against the backdrop of the St. Lawrence River. Here, beneath the canopy of the night sky, guests can mingle and converse in hushed tones, their laughter mingling with the gentle hum of the city below.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5000, -73.5675`
* **The Labyrinth of Old Montreal’s Alleys**: Narrow alleys wind between old stone buildings and hidden courtyards. At night, these dimly lit paths twist through the district, ending in secluded dead-ends or courtyards that lie in shadow, making the maze a dark, disorienting network.

* **Notre-Dame Basilica**: Towering with Gothic Revival architecture, intricate stone carvings, and two imposing bell towers. Inside, vaulted ceilings and ornate altars are dimly lit in blue and gold, creating an atmosphere of silence and awe within the echoing halls.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5048, -73.5560`
* **The Clock Tower and Promenade**: Rising at the harbor’s edge, this massive white tower offers sweeping views of the river and city skyline. At night, the clock’s light casts long shadows along the nearby promenade, blending with the soft rush of water along the shore.

## Mont Royal Plateau
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Rue Saint-Denis**: Stretching through the Plateau, Rue Saint-Denis is a lively avenue known for its historic facades and vibrant culture. The street is lined with boutiques, restaurants, and cafés, giving it a dynamic, bustling atmosphere. By day, it’s busy with pedestrians exploring shops and markets; by night, it transforms into a lively social scene, with patrons spilling out from bistros and bars.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5180, -73.5640`
* **Mount Royal Cemetery**: This sprawling, serene cemetery sits on the northern slope of Mount Royal, its winding paths lined with ancient trees and historic gravestones. Overlooking the city, the cemetery is both a green refuge and a place of history, with notable monuments and tombs of well-known figures. The quiet atmosphere and scenic views make it a place of introspection, offering a stark contrast to the city’s energy below.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5150, -73.6050`
    * **Graverobber DC**: 2
* **Carré Saint-Louis**: Nestled in the Plateau, this charming square is surrounded by ornate Victorian-style row houses and a blend of art galleries, bistros, and shops. The central fountain and tree-shaded benches invite visitors to linger, while artists and performers occasionally add to the lively atmosphere. Often referred to as one of Montreal’s most picturesque parks, it’s an iconic spot that captures the neighborhood's artistic and bohemian spirit.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5170, -73.5695`
* **Mount Royal Summit and Cross**: Atop Mount Royal, the city’s most famous landmark—the illuminated cross—stands tall. Visible from across Montreal, this cross marks the peak of Mount Royal and is a beloved symbol for residents. The summit offers panoramic views of the cityscape and surrounding areas, accessible by paths that wind up through the forested hillside, making it a popular destination for visitors and locals alike.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5039, -73.5878`
    * **Roadside Killer DC**: 3

## Little Italy
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Church of the Madonna della Difesa**: A striking brick church, its high, pointed arches and intricate stonework make it a dominating figure in the area. Inside, the vast nave is illuminated by vibrant stained-glass windows, casting colored light across the pews. Beneath, a crypt holds the remains of past generations, and the towering bell tower looms above the surrounding streets.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5348, -73.6120`
* **Nestor's Bar**: A hidden bar with a vintage, speakeasy vibe. The dimly lit interior is furnished with plush velvet and dark wood, giving it a quiet, intimate feel. The bar itself, an imposing counter made of polished wood, stands in the center, surrounded by softly glowing lights and tasteful decor. The space feels like it’s been untouched by time.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5350, -73.6110`
* **Little Italy Market (Jean-Talon Market)**: A sprawling market with rows of produce stalls, butcher counters, and flower vendors. The wide aisles and high ceilings give the space an open, airy feel, but the stone floors and brick walls add an element of weight and history. Outside, the market is surrounded by the low-rise buildings of the neighborhood, lending it a sense of connection to the community.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5361, -73.6149`
* **Evans Funeral House**: Combining modern minimalism with a serene atmosphere, Evans Funeral House’s lobby features soft neutral tones—muted greys, whites, and light wood. Large windows let in natural light, and plush seating is arranged around dark wood tables. The viewing rooms offer simple elegance, designed to feel peaceful and private, creating a calming environment for mourners.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5310, -73.6140`
    * **Portillon (Hecata leadership)**: ●●●●
* **Rialto Theatre**: An elegant, historic building with a faded art deco style. The grand lobby features marble floors, tall columns, and a massive chandelier. The main theatre is vast, with ornate seating and a stage framed by intricate detailing. The high ceiling is adorned with decorative patterns, and the atmosphere is one of old-world grandeur. 
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5232, -73.6050`

## Ahuntsic Cartierville
**General Hunting DC:** 6

* **Fleury Hospital**: Fleury Hospital, a modest yet longstanding institution, has an unassuming façade typical of a mid-20th-century design, with wide windows that reflect Montreal's gray skies and tall concrete pillars. Inside, the lighting is soft, though dim in places, casting a slightly eerie atmosphere over the aged linoleum floors and scuffed walls. Hallways are narrow and often empty in the evening hours, echoing the faint hum of machinery and the occasional muffled voices from staff. Rooms are simply furnished, with plain white walls that seem to absorb more than reflect light. The hospital exudes a functional, almost forgotten feel, making it an ideal place for hidden activities away from the bustling city.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5684, -73.6558`
    * **Bagger DC**: 3
    * **Grim Reaper DC**: 2
* **Saint-Benoît Church**: A historical church with impressive architecture that hides its age well. It’s largely empty at night, creating an atmosphere filled with mystery and solitude. Kindred sensitive to holy symbols may experience discomfort here, but its isolation might appeal to those seeking protection from prying eyes or as a meeting spot in the mortal world.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5450, -73.6550`
* **Ahuntsic-Cartierville Public Library**: The library building stands squat and unassuming, its architecture a blend of simple brick and faintly worn stonework. The entrance is framed by narrow, rectangular windows, dimly lit from within, giving a hint of the hushed sanctuary beyond. Overgrown ivy clings to one side, partially obscuring an old metal sign. Small, deliberate lights cast a faint glow around the base of the structure, barely holding back the shadows that settle on it as night falls—a humble yet enduring place, inviting only those who seek it out.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5440, -73.6600`
* **Parc Ahuntsic**: Parc Ahuntsics tretches out as a peaceful retreat amid the urban landscape, with its wide, winding paths framed by mature trees whose branches form canopies overhead. During the day, sunlight filters through the leaves, casting dappled shadows over park benches and picnic spots. In the evening, the park’s lampposts cast gentle pools of light along the walkways, while sections of the park grow quiet and dim, especially around the small lake in the center. The water reflects the moonlight at night, surrounded by a hint of mist, giving the area a slightly eerie calm, especially as the sounds of the city fade into the distance.
    * **Coordinates**: `45.5528, -73.6661`
